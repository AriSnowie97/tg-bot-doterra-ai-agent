# Intoxication Starts with Protein Deficiency (Chunk 23)

## Main Idea

Intoxication is not always an excess of toxins. Often, it is the inability of the liver to neutralize them.

The most dangerous toxins are not those entering the body from the outside, but those already processed by the liver yet remaining in the body—they accumulate in adipose tissue, especially around the abdomen. The larger the belly, the more toxins remain in the body.

## The Role of the Liver

The liver is responsible for neutralizing:
- metabolic products
- hormones
- medications
- xenobiotics

This process is called liver detox. The liver cannot neutralize toxins on its own—to do this, it needs amino acids, the building blocks of protein.

## What Happens During Protein Deficiency

- Phase I and Phase II of liver detoxification slow down
- toxins circulate longer
- the load on the nervous system increases
- chronic inflammation worsens

**Formula:** Liver detoxification phase = protein + B vitamins + trace elements. No protein—no detox.

## Why Protein Deficiency Occurs Even with Sufficient Intake

Protein deficiency occurs not only due to low intake—even in sufficient amounts, protein may be poorly absorbed.

Breaking down protein bonds requires sufficiently concentrated hydrochloric acid and the enzyme pepsin. With low stomach acidity, protein is not fully broken down.

**Causes of low stomach acidity:**
- chronic stress
- age-related changes
- zinc deficiency
- frequent use of PPIs (proton pump inhibitors) and antacids
- irregular eating habits

## Conclusion

The liver is ready for detoxification when it has enough resources. The body is a self-regulating system capable of healing itself if it is not hindered and is supported.
