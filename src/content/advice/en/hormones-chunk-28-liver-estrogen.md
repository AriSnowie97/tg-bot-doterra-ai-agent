# Liver and Hormonal Balance: The Connection with Estrogen, Bile, and Cortisol (Chunk 28)

> From the perspective of functional/integrative medicine — not a substitute for medical examination or prescribed treatment.

## The Liver — Regulator of Estrogen

A significant portion of estrogen metabolism occurs in the liver. When it is overloaded (diet, stress, toxins, medications), according to functional medicine claims, estrogen may be excreted more slowly and accumulate, exacerbating its relative excess.

Proponents of this approach link excess estrogen to an increased risk of cyst growth, fibroids, and manifestations of endometriosis — alongside other recognized factors (genetics, inflammation, hormonal disorders) that remain an important part of the full picture.

## Bile Flow and Hormonal Balance

Through bile, the liver excretes a portion of processed hormones. If bile flow is sluggish (sedentary lifestyle, lack of dietary fat, taurine or magnesium deficiency), according to this model, hormones may remain in the body longer.

A study cited by the source links chronic bile stasis to a higher incidence of cysts and cycle irregularities in certain samples of women.

## The Liver and Cortisol

Cortisol (an adrenal hormone), according to this model, can deplete liver glycogen and impair its detoxifying capacity, contributing to the accumulation of toxins and excess hormones. The source refers to a publication in the *Journal of Endocrinology* (2021) regarding the link between high cortisol levels and a reduced ability of the liver to metabolize estrogens.

**Simplified model of the connection (according to the source):** overloaded liver → hormones are excreted more slowly → relative estrogen excess → increased risk of cysts/nodules in the ovaries and breasts → stress and poor sleep worsen the picture.

## What Prolonged Imbalance Can Lead to (as Described by the Source)

- Recurrent cysts
- Cycle irregularities
- Difficulty conceiving
- Chronic fatigue, inflammation
- Worsening of skin, hair, and mood condition
