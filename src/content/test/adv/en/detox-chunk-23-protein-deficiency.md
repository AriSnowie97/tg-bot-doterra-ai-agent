# Intoxication begins with protein deficiency (chunk 23)

## Main idea

Intoxication is not always an excess of toxins. Often, it is the inability of the liver to neutralize them.

The most dangerous toxins are not those that enter the body from the outside, but those that have already been processed by the liver but remain in the body — they accumulate in adipose tissue, especially around the abdomen. The larger the belly, the more toxins remain in the body.

## The role of the liver

The liver is responsible for neutralizing:
- metabolic products
- hormones
- medications
- xenobiotics

This process is called liver detox. The liver cannot neutralize toxins on its own — it needs amino acids, the building blocks of protein, to do so.

## What happens during protein deficiency

- Phase I and Phase II liver detoxification slow down
- toxins circulate longer
- the burden on the nervous system increases
- chronic inflammation worsens

**Formula:** Liver detoxification phase = protein + B vitamins + trace elements. No protein — no detox.

## Why protein deficiency occurs even with adequate intake

Protein deficiency occurs not only due to low intake — even in adequate amounts, protein may be poorly absorbed.

Concentrated enough hydrochloric acid and the enzyme pepsin are required to break protein bonds. With reduced stomach acidity, protein is not fully broken down.

**Causes of reduced stomach acidity:**
- chronic stress
- age-related changes
- zinc deficiency
- frequent use of PPIs (proton pump inhibitors) and antacids
- irregular meals

## Conclusion

The liver is ready for detoxification when it has enough resources. The body is a self-regulating system capable of healing itself if it is not interfered with and is helped.
